\section{Results and discussion}

\subsection{Experimental Tm was the target and Tm-only the baseline}

Every model predicted experimental Tm for the same 396 held-out sequences, and
every setting was chosen on the Tm validation set
(Fig.~\ref{fig:learning_framework}); the computed labels entered only as
auxiliary training targets. We report each computed-label model against a
baseline trained on Tm alone in the same way, which we call the Tm-only model. A
change in mean absolute error ($\Delta$MAE) below zero therefore means the
computed labels lowered the Tm error. The Tm-only baseline reached
7.23$^\circ$C with frozen ESM2 and 6.55$^\circ$C with fine-tuned ESM2 in the
structure-matched comparison. The heterogeneous MD study was tuned separately
and had its own Tm-only baseline (7.38 and 6.61$^\circ$C), so each computed-label
model was compared only with the Tm-only baseline from its own study and encoder
setting.

\subsection{The local mutation scan reduced Tm error more than a heterogeneous set}

We first varied $n$, the number of computed labels sampled per structure, for
the local mutation scan label (Fig.~\ref{fig:data_design}a). With a frozen encoder,
$\Delta$MAE moved from $+0.09^\circ$C at $n=20$ to $-0.20^\circ$C at $n=320$,
where its 95\% interval lay below zero. With a fine-tuned encoder it began well
above the baseline ($+0.47^\circ$C at $n=20$; 95\% CI, $+0.26$ to
$+0.69^\circ$C) and improved as $n$ grew, reaching an interval that included zero
by $n=160$. In both encoders $\Delta$MAE fell as $n$ increased. The four points
are too few to establish a scaling relation, but the downward trend was
consistent.

We then compared two ways of building the MD label set
(Fig.~\ref{fig:data_design}b). The heterogeneous data set contained 1143 samples
representing 833 unique sequences of different lengths, whereas the local
mutation scan data set contained single mutations of the fixed 1MEL and 4IDL structures.
Both used the same 400~K MD simulations and the same Best--Hummer native-contact
$Q$ definition, with the same contact set and averaging window, so they differed
only in the variants they covered. Because the models for the two studies were
tuned separately, we compared each against its own Tm-only baseline. Adding the
MD label changed MAE by $-0.049^\circ$C with a frozen encoder (95\% CI,
$-0.092$ to $-0.006^\circ$C) and by $+0.116^\circ$C with a fine-tuned encoder
(95\% CI, $-0.009$ to $+0.243^\circ$C) in the heterogeneous study, and by
$-0.195^\circ$C (95\% CI, $-0.366$ to $-0.030^\circ$C) and $+0.029^\circ$C
(95\% CI, $-0.139$ to $+0.197^\circ$C) in the local mutation scan study. The
frozen-encoder reduction was therefore larger for the local mutation scans.

Eight sequences in the heterogeneous MD data set also occurred in the Tm test
set, but excluding their test errors barely changed either estimate (frozen
$-0.049^\circ$C, 95\% CI $-0.094$ to $-0.005^\circ$C; fine-tuned
$+0.122^\circ$C, 95\% CI $-0.005$ to $+0.248^\circ$C). We did not retrain after
removing the overlapping samples, so this check addresses their contribution to
the test MAE but not a possible effect during training or model selection.

The two data sets shared the same $Q$ definition, so their different effect on
the Tm error must come from the variants each covered. A local mutation scan holds the
structural background fixed, so each label (through $\Delta Q$) isolates the
local effect of a single mutation, the same kind of stability change the model
must learn for Tm.
The heterogeneous set instead compares many different nanobodies, each against
its own native structure, so its $Q$ reflects whole-protein differences such as
length and fold rather than the effect of a mutation. An auxiliary label tied to
protein identity rather than to a mutation's
thermodynamic effect is only loosely related to Tm, so it offers the shared
representation little useful signal and can bias it toward features that do not
generalize to the target. Such a mismatch between the auxiliary and target tasks
is a known source of negative transfer, where a poorly matched auxiliary task
fails to help or degrades performance \citep{Zhang2023NegativeTransfer}.
Here again, what mattered was how well the computed variants matched the
prediction task, not how many labels were available.

\subsection{FEP had the lowest test MAE with both encoders}

We then compared all seven computed labels, each with a model tuned separately
on the Tm validation set (Fig.~\ref{fig:physical_observable}). With a frozen encoder, FEP reached
7.01$^\circ$C MAE, a $\Delta$MAE of $-0.221^\circ$C from the Tm-only baseline
(95\% CI, $-0.393$ to $-0.051^\circ$C). The local mutation scan reached 7.03$^\circ$C
($\Delta$MAE $-0.195^\circ$C; 95\% CI, $-0.366$ to $-0.030^\circ$C), and both
intervals were below zero. The Rosetta labels were close to or worse than the
Tm-only baseline, and ThermoMPNN reached 7.09$^\circ$C with an interval that
included zero. With a fine-tuned encoder, FEP reached 6.40$^\circ$C, the lowest
MAE in the study; its $\Delta$MAE from the 6.55$^\circ$C Tm-only baseline was
$-0.153^\circ$C (95\% CI, $-0.323$ to $+0.020^\circ$C), an interval that included
zero. The local mutation scan, Rosetta, ThermoMPNN, and random variants scored with
Rosetta differed from Tm-only by $+0.029$ to $+0.144^\circ$C, with intervals
that also included zero. ESM2-proposed variants scored with Rosetta increased
error by $+0.411^\circ$C (95\% CI, $+0.140$ to $+0.698^\circ$C).

Comparing FEP and the local mutation scan directly, the difference in test MAE (FEP
minus MD) was $-0.026^\circ$C with a frozen encoder (95\% CI, $-0.243$ to
$+0.192^\circ$C) and $-0.182^\circ$C with a fine-tuned encoder (95\% CI,
$-0.393$ to $+0.024^\circ$C). Neither interval excluded zero, so the two labels
were not distinguishable at the largest label count.

FEP $\Delta\Delta G$ measures the free-energy effect of a mutation, whereas
high-temperature $Q$ measures contact retention over a finite trajectory. The
closer thermodynamic connection between $\Delta\Delta G$ and mutation stability
may help explain why FEP had a lower MAE after ESM2 fine-tuning,
although the present experiments do not test that mechanism. In a recent study,
we used sparse autoencoders to examine ESM2 after fine-tuning on nanobody Tm and
found features associated with surface charge, hydrophobic-core packing, the VHH
tetrad, and disulfide bonds \citep{Murakami2025Mechanistic}. We did not examine
the representations learned here. Because FEP $\Delta\Delta G$ is close to
the stability signal, we would expect it to strengthen the same features, and
comparing Tm-only, Tm+FEP, and Tm+MD models under one regression architecture
would test this directly.

\subsection{The results held under controls, but several limitations remain}

Without size-specific tuning, FEP reduced MAE relative to Tm-only for both the
35M- and 650M-parameter ESM2 models; both paired 95\% intervals were below zero
(Supplementary Fig.~S1). The larger models used fixed settings rather than the
search applied to the 8M model, so these tests do not compare performance across
model sizes. An analytical correction for periodic
images in charge-changing FEP mutations had almost no effect on the scaled
labels. Under the Rosetta protocol used here,
ESM2-proposed variants did not give lower Tm error than random variants. This
comparison provides no evidence that the ESM2 proposal step improved transfer
to Tm.
Data set size did not explain the contrast between the two MD studies. The
heterogeneous data set contained 1143 samples and the local mutation scan data set contained
837, but each ensemble member sampled at most 640 auxiliary samples in both
studies.
The larger frozen-encoder reduction for the local mutation scans therefore
cannot be explained by more training labels per model. FEP and the local mutation
scan required similar simulation time per variant, but wall-clock costs were not
recorded consistently enough to support a general cost comparison.

The study has several limitations. The Tm training set contains only 57
nanobodies, and the local mutation scan calculations cover two structures. Although the two
MD data sets now share one $Q$ definition, the heterogeneous data set still
differs from the local mutation scans in its variant composition. It includes repeated
PDB entries, eight exact matches to the Tm test sequences, and sequences of
widely varying length, and its trajectories did not all reach the scheduled
production length. The selected FEP and MD models also differed in regression
architecture and in whether the auxiliary loss updated the encoder. The
bootstrap intervals resample the fixed test proteins after predictions are
averaged across trained models. They do not include uncertainty from selecting
auxiliary samples, training seeds, or model settings. Finally, we did not make new Tm measurements or
evaluate predictions on newly measured nanobodies.
