\section{Results and discussion}

\subsection{Models were selected using experimental Tm error}

All comparisons used experimental Tm as the target and the same 396-sequence
test set; computed labels entered only as auxiliary training targets
(Fig.~\ref{fig:learning_framework}). For each data source, model settings were
selected with the experimental Tm validation set. The Tm-only test MAE was
7.23$^\circ$C with frozen ESM2 and 6.55$^\circ$C with fine-tuned ESM2 in the
structure-matched comparison. The separately selected Tm-only references for
the heterogeneous study had MAEs of 7.38 and 6.61$^\circ$C,
respectively. Each computed-label model was compared only with the Tm-only
model selected in the same study and encoder setting.

\subsection{The frozen-encoder MAE reduction was numerically larger for matched scans}

We compared two ways of building an MD label set. The heterogeneous data set
contained 1143 samples representing 833 unique sequences of different lengths.
The matched data set contained local single mutations of the fixed 1MEL and 4IDL
structures. Both used the same 400~K MD and Best--Hummer native-contact $Q$
protocol, with the same contact set and averaging window, so they differed only
in the variants they covered. The models for the two studies were tuned
separately, so we compared the change from the Tm-only model within each study
rather than their absolute MAEs (Fig.~\ref{fig:data_design}a). In the heterogeneous study, adding the MD
label changed MAE by $-0.049^\circ$C with a frozen encoder (95\% CI,
$-0.092$ to $-0.006^\circ$C) and by $+0.116^\circ$C with a fine-tuned encoder
(95\% CI, $-0.009$ to $+0.243^\circ$C). In the matched study, the corresponding
changes were $-0.195^\circ$C (95\% CI, $-0.366$ to $-0.030^\circ$C) and
$+0.029^\circ$C (95\% CI, $-0.139$ to $+0.197^\circ$C). The frozen-encoder MAE
reduction was therefore larger for the matched mutation scans.

Eight sequences in the heterogeneous MD data set also occurred in the Tm test
set. Excluding their test errors barely changed either estimate, and the paired
changes were $-0.049^\circ$C for the frozen encoder (95\% CI, $-0.094$ to
$-0.005^\circ$C) and $+0.122^\circ$C for the fine-tuned encoder (95\% CI,
$-0.005$ to $+0.248^\circ$C). We did not retrain after removing the overlapping
computed-label samples, so this check addresses their contribution to test MAE
but not a possible effect during training or model selection. Other differences
between the data sets may also matter. In the heterogeneous data set, $Q$ had a
Pearson correlation of $r=+0.13$ with sequence length
(Supplementary Fig.~S1). The matched scans held length fixed within each
structure and described the local effect of one mutation. The heterogeneous data
set also included each structure separately, giving more weight to sequences
represented by multiple structures. These observations do not identify the cause
of the difference.

The frozen-encoder label-count curves provide a second comparison of FEP and
matched MD (Fig.~\ref{fig:data_design}b). At $n=20$, the paired MAE changes were
$-0.10^\circ$C for FEP and $+0.09^\circ$C for matched MD. At $n=320$, they were
$-0.22^\circ$C and $-0.20^\circ$C, respectively, and both 95\% intervals were
below zero. The direct FEP-minus-MD difference at $n=320$ was
$-0.03^\circ$C (95\% CI, $-0.24$ to $+0.19^\circ$C), which did not resolve a
difference between the labels. Four label counts, with nonmonotonic changes,
are too few to establish a scaling relation.

\subsection{FEP had the lowest test MAE with both encoders}

We next compared all computed labels after selecting the model for each source
separately (Fig.~\ref{fig:physical_observable}a). With a frozen encoder, FEP
reached 7.01$^\circ$C MAE, a paired change of $-0.221^\circ$C from Tm-only
(95\% CI, $-0.393$ to $-0.051^\circ$C). Matched MD reached
7.03$^\circ$C, a change of $-0.195^\circ$C (95\% CI, $-0.366$ to
$-0.030^\circ$C). Both intervals were below zero. ThermoMPNN reached
7.09$^\circ$C, but its interval included zero. The Rosetta-based labels were
close to or worse than the Tm-only model. With a fine-tuned encoder, FEP reached
6.40$^\circ$C, the lowest MAE in the
study. Its paired change from the 6.55$^\circ$C Tm-only model
was $-0.153^\circ$C (95\% CI, $-0.323$ to $+0.020^\circ$C), an interval that
included zero. Matched MD, ThermoMPNN, plain Rosetta, and random variants scored
with Rosetta differed from Tm-only by $+0.029$ to $+0.144^\circ$C; their
intervals also included zero. ESM2-proposed variants scored with Rosetta
increased error by $+0.411^\circ$C (95\% CI, $+0.140$ to $+0.698^\circ$C).

The direct FEP-minus-MD comparison is shown in
Fig.~\ref{fig:physical_observable}b. Negative values favor FEP. The difference
was $-0.026^\circ$C with a frozen encoder (95\% CI, $-0.243$ to
$+0.192^\circ$C) and $-0.182^\circ$C with a fine-tuned encoder (95\% CI,
$-0.393$ to $+0.024^\circ$C). Neither interval excluded zero at the largest
label count.
The fine-tuned label-count curves show how the two results changed with the
amount of auxiliary data (Fig.~\ref{fig:physical_observable}c). At $n=20$,
matched MD increased MAE by $+0.47^\circ$C (95\% CI, $+0.26$ to
$+0.69^\circ$C). Its error decreased as $n$ increased, and the interval
included zero by $n=160$. FEP changed from $+0.02^\circ$C at $n=20$ to
$-0.15^\circ$C at $n=320$, but the interval at the largest setting also
included zero. These four label counts show the direction of the changes but
are too few to establish a scaling relation.

FEP $\Delta\Delta G$ measures the free-energy effect of a mutation, whereas
high-temperature $Q$ measures contact retention over a finite trajectory. The
closer thermodynamic connection between $\Delta\Delta G$ and mutation stability
may help explain why FEP had a lower MAE after ESM2 fine-tuning,
although the present experiments do not test that mechanism. In a recent
preprint, we used sparse autoencoders to examine ESM2 after fine-tuning on
nanobody Tm and found features associated with surface charge, hydrophobic-core
packing, the VHH tetrad, and disulfide bonds
\citep{Murakami2025Mechanistic}. We did not examine the representations learned
here. A useful follow-up would be to test whether FEP supervision strengthens
the same features by comparing Tm-only, Tm+FEP, and Tm+MD models with the same
regression architecture.

\subsection{Additional checks and limitations}

Without size-specific tuning, FEP reduced MAE relative to Tm-only for both the
35M- and 650M-parameter ESM2 models; both paired 95\% intervals were below zero
(Supplementary Fig.~S2a). The larger models used fixed settings rather than the
search applied to the 8M model, so these tests do not compare performance across
model sizes. An analytical correction for periodic
images in charge-changing FEP mutations had almost no effect on the scaled
labels (Supplementary Fig.~S2b). Under the Rosetta protocol used here,
ESM2-proposed variants did not give lower Tm error than random variants. This
comparison provides no evidence that the ESM2 proposal step improved transfer
to Tm.
Data set size did not explain the contrast between the two MD studies. The
heterogeneous data set contained 1143 samples and the matched data set contained
837, but each ensemble member sampled at most 640 auxiliary samples in both
studies.
The larger frozen-encoder reduction for the matched scans therefore
cannot be explained by more training labels per model. FEP and matched
MD required similar simulation time per variant, but wall-clock costs were not
recorded consistently enough to support a general cost comparison.

The study has several limitations. The Tm training set contains only 57
nanobodies, and the matched calculations cover two structures. Although the two
MD data sets now share one $Q$ protocol, the heterogeneous data set still
differs from the matched scans in its variant composition. It includes repeated
PDB entries, eight exact matches to the Tm test sequences, and sequences of
widely varying length, and its trajectories did not all reach the scheduled
production length. The selected FEP and MD models also differed in regression
architecture and in whether the auxiliary loss updated the encoder. The
bootstrap intervals resample the fixed test proteins after predictions are
averaged across trained models. They do not include uncertainty from selecting
auxiliary samples, training seeds, or model settings. Finally, we did not make new Tm measurements or
evaluate predictions on newly measured nanobodies.
